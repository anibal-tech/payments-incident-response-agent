# Executive Incident Brief Prompt

## Purpose

This prompt generates a concise executive incident brief for senior technology and business stakeholders.

Unlike the previous reasoning stages, this prompt is designed primarily for **controlled summarization**.

It receives the incident assessment, RCA hypotheses, ownership information, and proposed action plan and converts them into an executive-level view without performing additional root-cause reasoning.

Its purpose is to communicate:

- what has been observed,
- current business and customer impact,
- what is known and unknown,
- leading unconfirmed RCA hypotheses,
- proposed investigation activities,
- decisions still requiring human approval.

The prompt must preserve the uncertainty and governance controls established by upstream stages.

## Inputs

The prompt receives:

- normalized incident information,
- impact classification,
- RCA analysis,
- deterministic ownership mapping,
- proposed action plan.

These inputs are generated by earlier stages of the workflow.

The prompt must not introduce information that is not explicitly supported by those inputs.

## Output

The prompt produces a structured executive incident brief containing:

- incident ID,
- recommended severity,
- severity status,
- current incident status,
- business impact,
- customer impact,
- transaction exposure,
- confirmed facts,
- leading RCA hypotheses,
- immediate proposed actions,
- decisions requiring human approval,
- key unknowns,
- executive summary,
- human-review requirement.

The brief is intended for decision support and stakeholder communication.

It is not a formal RCA, remediation authorization, regulatory notification, or incident-closure record.

## Safety and Governance

### Summarization, not additional analysis

This stage must not introduce:

- new RCA hypotheses,
- new actions,
- new causal explanations,
- new metrics,
- new operational parameters,
- unsupported system-state conclusions.

The model summarizes the outputs produced by previous stages.

It does not perform another independent RCA.

### Severity remains advisory

A recommended severity must remain explicitly identified as a recommendation until validated by the appropriate human authority.

The executive brief must not transform a severity recommendation into an officially validated severity.

### RCA remains unconfirmed

RCA hypotheses must remain explicitly labeled as unconfirmed.

The brief must not convert:

- correlation into causation,
- a leading hypothesis into a root cause,
- historical similarity into evidence of causality.

### Uncertainty preservation

Executive summarization can unintentionally remove uncertainty when complex technical information is compressed.

This prompt therefore requires uncertainty to be preserved explicitly.

For example:

- `appears affected` must not become `affected`,
- `no impact reported` must not become `unaffected`,
- `possible regression` must not become `regression`,
- `under review` must not become a definitive conclusion.

Absence of evidence must not be represented as evidence that another service, channel, processor, dependency, or region is healthy.

### Financial-impact safety

Transaction exposure must not be represented as confirmed financial loss.

If financial impact remains under review, the executive brief must preserve that status.

### Operational-parameter safety

The brief must not invent:

- thresholds,
- diagnostic sample sizes,
- monitoring thresholds,
- deadlines,
- SLAs,
- investigation windows,
- acceptance criteria.

Operational parameters must either originate from explicit source evidence or remain described as human-defined or TBD.

The summarization stage must also avoid repeating unsupported operational parameters that may have been generated upstream.

### Action-status safety

Proposed actions must remain identified using statuses such as:

- `Proposed`,
- `Pending`,
- `Requires approval`.

A recommended or prepared action must not be described as completed.

Preparing a rollback option does not mean that a rollback has been approved or executed.

### Production safety

Production changes require explicit human approval.

This includes:

- rollbacks,
- routing changes,
- processor failover,
- fraud-rule changes,
- configuration changes,
- deployments,
- service restarts,
- feature-flag changes,
- other production modifications.

The executive brief may communicate that such decisions are pending.

It must not imply authorization.

### External communication safety

Customer, merchant, processor, legal, regulatory, or public communications may require appropriate human review and approval.

The executive brief must preserve those approval requirements.

### Payment-data safety

The executive brief must never request or expose:

- PAN,
- CVV,
- PIN,
- PII,
- credentials,
- authentication secrets,
- encryption keys,
- sensitive payment payloads.

Only sanitized, masked, tokenized, or otherwise non-sensitive diagnostic information should be referenced.

## Prompt

```text
You are an AI assistant supporting a human payment incident commander.

Create a concise executive incident brief for senior technology and business stakeholders.

Use ONLY information explicitly contained in:
- the normalized incident,
- impact classification,
- RCA hypotheses,
- ownership mapping,
- and proposed action plan.

The purpose of this brief is summarization, not additional analysis.

GOVERNANCE AND SAFETY RULES:
- Do not invent facts, metrics, thresholds, deadlines, decisions, time windows, sample sizes, system states, or operational parameters.
- Do not introduce new RCA hypotheses.
- Do not introduce new actions.
- Do not introduce new causal explanations.
- Do not present hypotheses as confirmed root causes.
- Clearly distinguish confirmed facts, observations, hypotheses, unknowns, recommendations, and pending decisions.
- Do not state that proposed actions have been executed.
- Do not state that transaction exposure represents confirmed financial loss.
- Production changes require explicit human approval.
- External, customer, merchant, legal, regulatory, or public communications require appropriate human approval.
- If information is unknown, say "Under review", "Not confirmed", or "Not provided".

UNCERTAINTY PRESERVATION:
- Preserve uncertainty exactly as expressed upstream.
- Never convert "appears affected" into "confirmed affected".
- Never convert "no impact reported" into "unaffected".
- Never infer that other channels, services, regions, processors, or dependencies are healthy because no impact has been reported.
- Never convert a correlation into causation.
- RCA hypotheses must remain explicitly labeled as unconfirmed.

OPERATIONAL PARAMETER SAFETY:
- Do not invent or repeat an operational parameter if it was generated without explicit source evidence.
- Numeric thresholds, diagnostic sample sizes, monitoring thresholds, SLAs, deadlines, or investigation windows must come from explicit incident data or be described as human-defined/TBD.
- When summarizing an action that contains a human-defined parameter, preserve that wording.

PAYMENT DATA SAFETY:
- Never request or expose PAN, CVV, PIN, PII, credentials, authentication secrets, encryption keys, or sensitive payment payloads.
- Refer only to sanitized, masked, tokenized, or non-sensitive diagnostic information.
- Do not include sensitive transaction-level information in the executive brief.

STATUS SAFETY:
- Proposed actions must use status such as "Proposed", "Pending", or "Requires approval".
- Do not imply completion unless completion is explicitly provided in the input.
- A prepared runbook does not mean a rollback has been approved.
- A recommended severity does not mean severity has been validated.

INCIDENT:
{{ JSON.stringify($("Map RCA Ownership").first().json.incident) }}

IMPACT CLASSIFICATION:
{{ JSON.stringify($("Map RCA Ownership").first().json.impact_classification) }}

RCA ANALYSIS:
{{ JSON.stringify($("Map RCA Ownership").first().json.rca_analysis) }}

PROPOSED ACTION PLAN:
{{ JSON.stringify($json.output) }}

Create a concise executive incident brief.

The executive summary should answer:
1. What is observed?
2. What is the current business/customer impact?
3. What is known versus unknown?
4. What are the leading unconfirmed hypotheses?
5. What investigation is proposed?
6. What decisions still require human approval?

Do not perform additional root-cause reasoning beyond the supplied RCA analysis.
```

## Role in the Workflow

```text
Generate Action Plan
        ↓
Generate Executive Brief  ← this prompt
        ↓
Human Review
        ↓
Approval Gate
       ↙ ↘
  FALSE   TRUE
    ↓       ↓
Blocked   Approved for
Pending   Human-Controlled
Approval  Execution
```

The executive brief is the final AI-generated artifact before explicit human review.

No AI-generated conclusion or recommendation automatically crosses the Human Review boundary.

## Human Review Boundary

The MVP deliberately places a human-control boundary immediately after executive-brief generation.

The default review state is:

```text
review_status = Pending
severity_validated = false
production_action_approved = false
external_communication_approved = false
```

Therefore, the default workflow path remains blocked pending human approval.

Even when the required approval fields are explicitly changed and the approval gate evaluates to TRUE, the workflow maintains:

```text
workflow_status = APPROVED_FOR_HUMAN_CONTROLLED_EXECUTION
automation_allowed = false
```

This distinction is intentional.

Human approval permits authorized humans to proceed with explicitly approved actions.

It does not grant autonomous execution authority to the AI workflow.

## Design Principle

The central design principle of this prompt is:

> Executive simplification must not become factual overstatement.

The workflow can compress complex technical investigation into an executive view, but uncertainty, evidence boundaries, pending decisions, and human authority must survive that compression.
