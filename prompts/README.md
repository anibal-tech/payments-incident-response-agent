# Prompts

This folder contains the production prompts used by the **Payments Incident Response Agent - MVP**.

The prompts are designed as a controlled reasoning pipeline for payment incident response. Each prompt has a specific responsibility and explicit safety boundaries.

The AI provides decision support. It does not replace the human incident commander, confirm root cause autonomously, authorize production changes, or execute remediation.

## Prompt Pipeline

```text
Normalized Incident
        ↓
classify-impact.md
        ↓
Impact Classification
        ↓
generate-rca-hypotheses.md
        ↓
Ranked RCA Hypotheses
        ↓
Deterministic Ownership Mapping
        ↓
generate-actions.md
        ↓
Proposed Action Plan
        ↓
executive-incident-brief.md
        ↓
Executive Incident Brief
        ↓
Human Review
        ↓
Approval Gate
```

The workflow deliberately separates generative reasoning, deterministic logic, and human authority.

---

## Prompt Responsibilities

### 1. `classify-impact.md`

Performs the initial impact assessment and recommends an incident severity.

Key responsibilities:

- assess business and customer impact,
- recommend SEV1, SEV2, or SEV3,
- preserve uncertainty,
- distinguish observations from confirmed facts,
- identify missing information,
- avoid interpreting transaction exposure as confirmed financial loss.

The severity is a recommendation only and requires human validation.

---

### 2. `generate-rca-hypotheses.md`

Generates ranked Root Cause Analysis hypotheses using incident evidence, impact classification, and historical incident references.

Key responsibilities:

- generate plausible investigation hypotheses,
- rank hypotheses by available evidence,
- distinguish supporting and contradicting evidence,
- preserve uncertainty,
- prevent correlation from becoming causation,
- prevent historical similarity from becoming proof,
- propose diagnostic validation steps,
- avoid invented diagnostic time windows,
- protect sensitive payment information.

All generated RCA hypotheses remain unconfirmed.

The formal root cause must be established through evidence and human investigation.

---

### 3. `generate-actions.md`

Transforms the incident assessment and RCA hypotheses into a prioritized action plan.

Actions are organized using:

- **P1** — immediate diagnostics, evidence collection, monitoring, and safe coordination,
- **P2** — deeper investigation, contingency preparation, and remediation readiness,
- **P3** — post-incident improvements, testing, observability, and governance.

Key responsibilities:

- propose investigation activities,
- use deterministic owner-role assignments,
- preserve RCA uncertainty,
- prevent invented thresholds, deadlines, sample sizes, or operational parameters,
- protect sensitive payment information,
- identify actions requiring human approval,
- prevent proposed actions from being represented as executed.

The AI proposes actions.

It does not authorize or execute them.

---

### 4. `executive-incident-brief.md`

Converts the available incident analysis into a concise executive brief for senior technology and business stakeholders.

This stage performs controlled summarization rather than additional root-cause analysis.

Key responsibilities:

- summarize observed impact,
- communicate known facts and key unknowns,
- present leading RCA hypotheses as unconfirmed,
- summarize proposed actions,
- identify decisions requiring human approval,
- preserve uncertainty during executive simplification,
- prevent transaction exposure from becoming reported financial loss,
- prevent recommendations from becoming reported actions.

The executive brief is the final AI-generated artifact before the Human Review boundary.

---

## Separation of Responsibilities

The MVP intentionally separates three types of responsibility.

```text
AI Reasoning
    ↓
Impact assessment
RCA hypothesis generation
Action recommendations
Executive summarization

Deterministic Workflow Logic
    ↓
Incident normalization
Ownership mapping
Approval-condition evaluation
Workflow-state routing

Human Authority
    ↓
Severity validation
Evidence review
RCA confirmation
Production-action approval
External-communication approval
Incident closure
```

This separation is a core architectural principle of the project.

An AI-generated recommendation must not become an operational decision simply because it was generated by the workflow.

## Shared Governance Principles

All prompts follow a common set of governance principles.

### 1. Human-in-the-loop

AI outputs are advisory.

Human authority remains responsible for:

- validating severity,
- confirming root cause,
- approving production remediation,
- approving external or regulatory communications,
- determining incident closure.

### 2. Evidence before conclusion

The prompts must not invent facts, telemetry, system behavior, technical mechanisms, or operational evidence.

Unknown information remains unknown until evidence is available.

### 3. Correlation is not causation

A deployment or configuration change preceding an incident may justify investigation.

It does not prove that the change caused the incident.

### 4. Preserve uncertainty

Expressions such as:

- `appears affected`,
- `possible`,
- `potential`,
- `reported`,
- `under review`,
- `not confirmed`

must retain their uncertainty throughout the workflow.

Executive summarization must not remove these distinctions.

### 5. No invented operational parameters

The AI must not fabricate:

- thresholds,
- sample sizes,
- percentages,
- monitoring limits,
- deadlines,
- SLAs,
- diagnostic windows,
- acceptance criteria.

When such parameters are unavailable, the workflow uses concepts such as:

- `human-defined threshold`,
- `approved diagnostic sample`,
- `appropriate investigation window`,
- `organization-defined SLA`,
- `TBD by incident commander`.

### 6. Payment-data safety

Prompts must not request, expose, reproduce, or recommend collecting sensitive payment or authentication information such as:

- PAN,
- CVV,
- PIN,
- PII,
- credentials,
- authentication secrets,
- encryption keys,
- sensitive payment payloads.

Transaction-level investigation must use sanitized, masked, tokenized, or otherwise non-sensitive diagnostic information.

### 7. Production changes require human approval

The AI workflow does not autonomously perform:

- production rollbacks,
- payment-routing changes,
- processor failover,
- fraud-rule changes,
- configuration changes,
- deployments,
- service restarts,
- feature-flag changes,
- other production modifications.

These actions require explicit human authorization.

## Human Review Boundary

After the executive incident brief is generated, the workflow reaches an explicit Human Review stage.

The MVP defaults to:

```text
review_status = Pending
severity_validated = false
production_action_approved = false
external_communication_approved = false
```

With these values, the approval gate follows the blocked path:

```text
Human Review
      ↓
Approval Gate
      ↓
FALSE
      ↓
Await Human Approval
      ↓
BLOCKED_PENDING_HUMAN_APPROVAL
```

Even when the required approval fields are explicitly changed and the approval gate evaluates to TRUE, the workflow maintains:

```text
workflow_status = APPROVED_FOR_HUMAN_CONTROLLED_EXECUTION
automation_allowed = false
```

Approval therefore represents authorization for **human-controlled execution**, not autonomous AI execution.

## Prompt Design Strategy

The prompts intentionally become more constrained as information moves toward operational decision-making.

```text
Impact Classification
        ↓
Assess

RCA Hypotheses
        ↓
Investigate

Action Plan
        ↓
Recommend

Executive Brief
        ↓
Summarize

Human Review
        ↓
Decide
```

This progression reduces the risk that generative reasoning is mistaken for operational authority.

## Important

The prompts in this folder are part of an educational and portfolio MVP.

Incident data and historical cases included in the repository are fictional and sanitized.

A production implementation would require additional controls, security review, organizational policies, access management, auditability, monitoring integrations, and payment-industry compliance requirements appropriate to the operating environment.
